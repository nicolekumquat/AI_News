HSP GRUPPE itself is a network of legally independent tax advisory, auditing, and law firms. Reinventing professional work For more than two decades, HSP GRUPPE ⁠ (opens in a new window) has consistently invested in digitizing and standardizing professional firm processes. Long before generative AI, the firm network standardized processes, embedded quality management, and fostered a culture of continuous improvement across the network. When ChatGPT emerged, HSP saw more than another productivity tool. It saw an opportunity to rethink how professional work itself could evolve. Rather than asking how AI could help employees write faster emails or summarize documents, leadership asked a broader question: What happens when AI becomes part of the operating model of a professional services firm? That vision led HSP to embed ChatGPT Enterprise into tax advisory, legal research, client communication, financial analysis, and knowledge sharing across the firm network. “Technology is moving faster than organizations can transform. Our challenge wasn’t introducing AI—it was helping the organization absorb it.” —Carsten Schulz, CEO, HSP GRUPPE Inside the rollout HSP approached AI as an organizational transformation rather than a software rollout. The organization introduced ChatGPT Enterprise with a strong focus on adoption, governance, and continuous learning. Monthly AI forums gave employees a place to share practical use cases and learn from one another. ChatGPT Enterprise’s security features provide an important technical foundation, while the use of client-related information is governed by HSP’s internal data protection, confidentiality, and governance requirements. Professional review and final responsibility always remain with the relevant tax, legal, or accounting professional. The goal wasn’t simply to encourage experimentation. It was to build an organizational capability that could scale. As Carsten Schulz puts it: “People ask me what my favorite AI feature is. I never answer that question. Individual features don’t matter. Once you understand how everything fits together, AI opens the door to a completely new world.” Across the firm network, professionals quickly found new ways to apply AI to their daily work, while the organization also began standardizing successful use cases into custom Agents. One example is AI Client Communication , a shared Agent that supports first drafts, structure, clarity, and a more consistent, client-oriented tone. Professional review and final responsibility remain with the relevant tax, legal or accounting specialist. Another is the Booking Assistant SKR03 & SKR04 , which supports the preparation and classification of specific booking questions. The final professional decision remains with the employee. Together, these kinds of shared Agents reduce repetitive work while making best practices available to every employee—not just AI experts. Professionals then build on those foundations in their own work. Managing Partner Frank Heibel uses ChatGPT as a technical sparring partner to work through complex tax questions and refine client communications. “ChatGPT has become a genuine sparring partner. It completely changes the level at which you can work.” he says. Partner Magdalene Posnak reduced the time required to evaluate multiple real estate investments from nine hours to around two, allowing her to spend more time advising clients. Managing Director Marco Sell uses AI-powered dashboards to help clients explore financial scenarios together during meetings, while lawyer and tax advisor Jan-Henrik Leifelt uses ChatGPT to strengthen—not replace—his professional judgment. The common thread is simple: less time spent preparing information, and more time spent applying expertise. “An analysis of several real estate investments used to take me around nine hours. With ChatGPT, I can prepare it in about two—and use the time saved for client advisory.” —Magdalene Posnak, Partner, HSP GRUPPE Preparing for the next wave with ChatGPT Work HSP is already preparing for the next phase of its AI journey with ChatGPT Work. Just weeks after gaining access, the organization began piloting the platform with a small group of developers and administrators before expanding across the wider workspace. The goal is to understand how agentic AI can safely automate complex workflows while balancing governance, quality, and cost before broader rollout. For HSP, the economic benefit is not about reducing headcount. The firms in the network are already operating at capacity and managing substantial backlogs. Time saved with ChatGPT can therefore be redirected toward additional client work, shorter turnaround times, more advisory capacity, and stronger client service. For Schulz, the opportunity isn’t simply helping people complete existing tasks more quickly. It’s redesigning the workflow itself. He points to year-end accounting as an example. Today, accountants often discover missing information only when they begin preparing annual accounts months after bookkeeping has been completed. HSP is exploring how ChatGPT Work could continuously review bookkeeping, identify missing information throughout the year, and proactively request documents from clients—so that much of the preparation has already happened before an accountant even opens the file. That shift—from AI assisting people to AI orchestrating work across entire processes—represents the next stage of HSP’s transformation. “We identified 88 opportunities for automation. But I think that’s already thinking too small. The real question isn’t how we automate today’s processes. It’s how we redesign them. That’s what opens the door to a completely new world.” —Carsten Schulz, CEO, HSP GRUPPE Results at a glance Achieved 84% weekly active usage, with 755 weekly active users and 913 unique users over six months (February 1 to July 14, 2026). Leadership lessons Treat AI as an organizational capability, not an individual tool. Scale successful experiments into repeatable workflows that everyone can benefit from. Regular AI forums helped successful ideas spread quickly across the organization. Redesign workflows—not just tasks. The greatest value comes from rethinking end-to-end processes rather than automating isolated activities. Build AI around professional judgement. The greatest value comes when AI enhances expertise rather than replacing it. Tips Start with business problems—not AI features. Pilot new capabilities with small groups before expanding organization-wide. Measure adoption and business impact—not just deployment—to understand where AI is creating real value. As ChatGPT Work and agentic capabilities mature, HSP plans to move beyond assisting individual professionals toward automating entire workflows that proactively support employees and clients alike. For Schulz, the long-term opportunity extends beyond HSP itself. He believes AI can strengthen—not diminish—the role of trusted professionals by enabling them to spend more time exercising judgment, solving complex problems, and building client relationships. “Everyone talks about AI replacing tax advisors. August 6, 2026 Company Working with the American Psychological Association on youth mental health and AI Loading… Young people already use AI to learn, create, ask questions, and seek advice. As that use grows, families, schools, clinicians, and communities need clearer evidence, better , and stronger safeguards. That’s why we’re working with the American Psychological Association (APA) to bring psychological science into how we think about responsible AI development and use among young people. APA is the leading scientific and professional organization for psychology in the United States, whose evidence-grounded work will help clarify what is known, what is uncertain, and what responsible AI should look like as the technology evolves. “ Young people are engaging with AI during some of the most consequential years of their development. APA brings both the developmental science and clinical expertise to say what responsible design looks like and what matters most for protecting and promoting young people’s well-being. This work is about making sure that knowledge reaches the people building these systems. ” “Technology is part of teens’ lives, and our responsibility is to meet that reality with experiences that are safe, age-appropriate, and designed with families in mind. AI should strengthen—not replace—the real-world relationships and care young people rely on,” said Dr. Sara Johansen, head of mental health and well-being product policy at OpenAI. “This work builds on our ongoing efforts with experts. By working with the APA and other leading experts, we’re continuing to ground our approach in evidence and develop practical safeguards that put teens’ well-being first.” Areas of focus Earlier this year, we co-hosted a productive convening with the APA that brought together leaders from mental health organizations, researchers, clinicians, educators, and youth representatives to discuss where young people face gaps in support today, and where AI may responsibly help people find information and connect to real-world support. Building on these conversations, we expect this work to span several areas—including how AI can support young people in moments of distress, how it can be designed to be more developmentally appropriate and useful, and how parents, caregivers, and clinicians can be better equipped to guide young people’s AI use. Throughout, this work will be grounded in the science of human behavior and the voices and lived experiences of parents, caregivers, teachers, and young people themselves. Over time, that work can help inform stronger safeguards and evidence-informed approaches to how AI should show up in young people’s lives. Supporting parents and caregivers One early focus is giving parents and caregivers practical tools for navigating AI use at home, especially as teens start using these tools. Working with APA, we plan to develop family-facing to help parents and caregivers support healthy use, recognize when adult intervention may be appropriate, and reinforce AI as a tool, not a replacement for people or care. We intend to make this guidance broadly accessible across school communities as well. Supporting practitioners and the broader field We are also working with APA to support practitioners who are increasingly fielding questions about AI. Planned work includes for clinicians and school psychologists focused on promoting healthy AI usage, recognizing overreliance, and intervening in unhealthy use patterns. We will also continue convening leaders across healthcare, education, research, policy, technology, and youth advocacy to translate emerging lessons into practical guidance for the broader field. Listening to youth and understanding system gaps It is essential that these efforts are grounded in and shaped by those with lived experience, not based on assumptions. We will work with APA to convene teens, families, clinicians, school psychologists, and others to help us better understand how young people are using AI, how AI-related questions are showing up in schools, therapy, and family settings, where current systems are falling short, and where AI may responsibly help with access, education, or navigation. Our broader safety work This work builds on OpenAI’s efforts to improve how ChatGPT responds in sensitive moments and supports younger users. We work with more than 260 mental health experts to help ChatGPT better recognize signs of distress, respond with care, and guide people toward real-world support. We have also expanded access to localized crisis and one-click hotlines, added reminders to take a break, and introduced additional protections for younger users, including parental controls, notifying parents of safety concerns, etc., under-18 principles in our Model Spec, and an age-prediction model that helps apply the right experience and safeguards. Our long-term commitment AI may help people find information and navigate systems earlier, but young people’s well-being depends on strong human relationships and trustworthy support. We’re glad to be working with the APA, alongside the many researchers, clinicians, and educators we continue to learn from, as we work to make sure AI truly supports young people and those who care for, support, and guide them. Relying solely on closed models is not inherently safe: they can be breached, misused, or fail in ways that outsiders cannot detect. And concentrating advanced AI capabilities behind a small number of closed models compounds that risk. It results in a small number of single points of failure, weakens competition, and leaves critical technology in the hands of a few providers. Open weight models, on the other hand, allow a broad community of researchers and developers to examine their behavior, identify vulnerabilities, develop safeguards, and improve them over time. The one surprising note in the letter is that it comes out in support of distillation, where models train on output from other models: In shaping this ecosystem, policymakers should be careful not to conflate legitimate model-development techniques with misappropriation. Distillation, or the practice of using one model’s outputs to help train or improve another, is a widely used technique for model improvement, evaluation, and validation. It reflects a long tradition of learning from, building upon, and improving existing technologies, a tradition that has helped drive innovation since the rise of the open-source software movement.
