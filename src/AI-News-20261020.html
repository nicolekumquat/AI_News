The standard he describes is demanding: the solution must solve a real everyday problem and be direct, simple, and memorable. Technology serves as the means, never as the centerpiece of the experience. There is demand for this. A study by Itaú in partnership with Grupo Consumoteca shows that 80% of Brazilians want to better organize their financial lives and seek more effective support for decisions, and that 65% would like technologies that act as a financial copilot, providing ongoing guidance. “OpenAI’s models were very important in delivering this experience, because they were able to operate across this range of solutions.” —Carlos Eduardo Mazzei, CTO, Itaú ia.i: from convenience to advice, in one place The journey began in 2024, when the bank introduced the first Inteligência Itaú capabilities for specific financial needs. ia.i is the next stage: a complete conversational layer, activated by text or voice, bringing interactions related to checking accounts, credit, cards, insurance, and other services into one environment — and connecting customers to specialized human support whenever needed. This breadth was precisely what guided the choice of models. “OpenAI’s models were very important in delivering this experience, because they were able to operate across this range of solutions,” Mazzei explains. “From the simplest ones, which need to be efficient in cost and latency, to more complex solutions, where reasoning and all the necessary grounding are required to provide an appropriate answer.” Having a range of models — rather than a single model — made it possible to deliver the complete solution in one product. At the center of this experience is Customer DNA, a layer of financial context that helps the bank understand the needs, habits, and life stages of approximately 70 million customers. Under governance and responsible AI controls, GPT models are among the components that help maintain and enrich this representation, making interactions progressively more relevant. The solution rests on three principles: interactivity, multimodality — text, voice, image, and clicks in the same experience — and individualization. With approximately 300,000 customers in the first phase, the bank entered a period of learning about the real relationship between customer and product. “We can understand how customers begin to use this product more deeply and see how it can directly benefit each person’s daily financial life,” says Mazzei. Its presence extends across the journey: from a question about an expense in an account statement to investment advice and convenient transactions on WhatsApp. “The financial world often has products that are not so straightforward and need this context, this simpler relationship with money.” —Carlos Eduardo Mazzei, CTO, Itaú IARA: the layer that makes scale possible Behind the visible experiences is an architectural decision. To operate AI at the scale of a bank, Itaú built IARA — a governance and orchestration platform that gives teams controlled access to different models in line with internal policies. OpenAI is among the model providers available in this environment. This layer supports solutions such as the legal department’s, where the platform automates document reading, analysis, and interpretation. The solution operates on a repository of more than 90 million documents and executes more than 2 million tasks per month, with efficiency gains of 20% to 80% in stages of the department’s processes. “It is essential that this information be preserved, remain within the bank’s context, and access models in a secure, governed way, with an understanding of token-use efficiency so that the entire solution is economically viable for the bank,” Mazzei says. IARA addresses three problems at once: cost control per token, accuracy of responses, and application of responsible AI policies. Sullivan & Cromwell partner John Savva developed Agreement Analyzer as a single-user proof of concept built with Codex. He then collaborated with OpenAI on improving the quality of outputs, enhancing efficiency through parallel processing and transforming the application into a tool that could be used by multiple lawyers at the same time. Agreement Analyzer integrates Sullivan & Cromwell’s workflows, curated firm precedents, negotiating playbooks and ChatGPT Enterprise. After inputting a draft agreement into the tool, a lawyer can add their own selected precedents and a playbook with firm and client positions, as well as information on market practice. The tool performs multiple review cycles from different perspectives, synthesizes the outputs, helps identify potential issues and returns a memorandum and proposed tracked changes for a lawyer to review, adapt or reject. “It’s not about replacing lawyers. It’s about providing a tool for lawyers to do deeper analyses and find more authority and precedents to provide an even higher quality product for their clients.” —John Savva, Partner, Sullivan & Cromwell Making sources and uncertainty visible Savva emphasizes that what makes an AI tool’s output trustworthy is full transparency. “If an AI tool makes a claim about market practice, you need to be able to see the source of that information,” he says. Savva describes an application in which agents develop conclusions and other agents challenge them in an adversarial process. In a potential use case, specialized agents review the agreement in parallel for such things as market deviations and risk allocation, interactions between clauses, and ambiguities that could impact litigation. The results distinguish findings supported after challenge by other agents, dismissed findings, and unverified issues retained with qualifications. Lawyers can inspect the issues, their supporting explanations, and the relevant precedents, then decide which findings warrant changes in the final markup. What’s next Sullivan & Cromwell is continuing to explore tools shaped around specific legal tasks. For Savva, stronger analytical capabilities create an opportunity to support more of the research and writing process. Savva points to advances in reasoning capabilities with GPT‑6 Astra: “Astra’s really moved things forward in terms of the quality of the reasoning that’s available, and that has been the bottleneck in the past,” he says.
