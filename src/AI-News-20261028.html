The company started with the insight that legal teams are unusually time crunched, meaning some valuable work doesn’t get done. Think of the junior lawyer told to review an impossible number of contracts overnight and only able to focus on a fraction. Legora can provide those services “on tap”, which is why Junestrand’s ambition is that every piece of legal work in the world should run on his platform. OpenAI has been part of that journey from the beginning: the release of GPT‑3.5 was the moment many of the legal use cases the founding team had been experimenting with became possible. Today, OpenAI is a key model provider for Legora, powering core workflows across its legal AI platform, from agentic legal work to large-scale document review. Legora builds on new OpenAI model capabilities as they become available, translating advances in intelligence into what lawyers can accomplish with its product. “ The world needs more legal services and legal intelligence. With Legora, we get that on tap. ” An AI-native company building an AI-native product Legora’s use of AI goes beyond the product it sells, and has expanded its use of ChatGPT Work, Codex and dots across its own teams. OpenAI’s technology is becoming part of both the product Legora builds for its customers as well as shaping how the company itself operates. “It’s incredibly important to me that we stay as AI-native as possible,” confirms Junestrand. For Jacob Lauritzen, CTO at Legora, coding agents have changed how quickly the engineering team can develop new ideas. Instead of researching one possible solution and committing engineering time before knowing whether it’s the right path, teams can prototype many approaches at once. “We can explore 20 solutions at the same time and, within days, pick the one we know will be the best.” The same approach changes how Legora responds when something goes wrong. Previously, diagnosing a product issue could mean several engineers investigating different possible causes over a number of hours. Coding agents can now search millions of lines of logs and pursue many possible explanations simultaneously, sometimes taking Legora from bug report to working fix in around five minutes . Customers, of course, don’t see the engineering efficiency itself. But they do experience the results: problems can be resolved faster, feedback can reach product teams sooner, and new ideas can be tested with customers earlier in the development cycle. “ We can go to customers much more quickly with feedback. It enables us to build a better product faster. ” Giving Legora’s own lawyers room to do higher-value work Legora’s own legal team experiences the same transformation its product is designed to create for customers. General Counsel Emma Kadri Bergström points to the example of NDAs. Her team analysed the agreements they had historically signed and built a playbook that allows Legora to take a first pass at incoming NDAs, highlighting terms that fall outside acceptable parameters. Before the workflow was introduced, Kadri Bergström estimates that reviewing these “boring documents” consumed around 20% of the legal team’s capacity . Today, it’s under 1% . That reclaimed time matters because the company itself is scaling rapidly. In the past year, Kadri Bergström says the business has expanded from three offices to 18 , completed three financing rounds and made five acquisitions . The legal team has had to support that growth while remaining closely connected to the business. AI has also allowed the team to take on work that might previously have been outsourced or narrowed because of limited capacity. In M&A diligence, for example, agents can begin reviewing contracts and surfacing information across the deal set, with lawyers reviewing and interpreting the output. “AI doesn’t just make you faster. It also increases quality.” —Emma Kadri Bergström, General Counsel, Legora From faster review to broader, higher-quality analysis For Legora’s customers, the opportunity isn’t simply to complete the same work faster. AI has also increased the scope of work that can be taken on. Alex Fortescue-Webb, Global Head of Legal Engineering, points to due diligence as an example. In a traditional review, time and cost constraints can force teams to concentrate deeply on only the most valuable contracts. Legora’s Tabular Review workflow can analyse thousands of contracts across multiple questions in minutes, allowing lawyers to apply detailed scrutiny much more broadly. That can alter the quality of the final work. Risks that might previously have sat in contracts outside the manually reviewed subset can now be surfaced for a lawyer to assess. The results are already measurable. Junestrand cites one Fortune 100 customer that has: reduced contract turnaround time by 90% saved more than $20 million on external litigation and diligence returned 9.5 hours per lawyer each week for higher-value work “ AI has fundamentally changed both the speed at which you can do existing tasks and the breadth of analysis you can apply. ” Through the Legora plugin for ChatGPT ⁠ , legal teams can also use Legora’s capabilities within their ChatGPT conversations . Leadership lessons Lead by using. When more than 75% of leaders actively use Legora, median usage across the organisation is 3× higher. Start with the problem. Focus on what people need to accomplish, then determine where AI can improve the workflow. Build a network of champions. Internal advocates help turn experimentation into sustained adoption. Be ready to rebuild. As models improve, yesterday’s product assumptions can quickly become outdated. Design for trust. Build evaluation, testing and transparency into the experience from the start. What’s next For Legora, the next frontier isn’t just making today’s legal workflows faster. It’s expanding the kinds of work AI can take on, while making collaboration between lawyers and AI increasingly natural. Jacob Lauritzen sees two advances happening in parallel. First, agents will be able to tackle longer-running tasks that require far greater context and autonomy. He imagines due diligence spanning millions of pages running overnight, with the analysis ready for a lawyer to review when they arrive the next morning. At the same time, increasingly capable models are becoming faster. That opens up a different kind of experience: real-time collaboration where a lawyer and AI can work back and forth on a contract, analysis or piece of advice rather than treating AI as a tool that receives a task and returns an answer. “When you blend human and AI, that’s when the output comes out best.” —Jacob Lauritzen, CTO, Legora That moving frontier means Legora’s roadmap can’t be static. “Model improvements force me to reconsider the roadmap on a biweekly basis,” says Lauritzen. Engineers, product managers, designers and sales teams all frequently ask him what’s the new thing that’s become possible for customers. For Bergström, it’s about changing the perception of legal teams. “Every legal department is worried about becoming a bottleneck. That’s when the business stops coming to you,” she says. AI is transforming legal teams from bottleneck to business partner, from the people you avoid to the people you want in the room alongside you. If Legora succeeds, the outcome won’t simply be lawyers completing today’s workload more efficiently. It will be legal teams able to take on work that capacity once made impossible, with more time for judgement, strategy and the high-value decisions that still require a human. Retell built its vCX benchmark to compare dozens of models using difficult, multi-turn conversations drawn from real production scenarios. It tests how well models follow instructions, stay grounded in the information they are given, call the right tools, and use natural conversational language. In Retell’s October 13, 2026 benchmark, GPT‑6 Astra ranked first for instruction following at 89.7%. Retell also shared evaluations conducted before the launch of the GPT‑6 Astra model that showed GPT‑5.5 ranking highest among models on both non-hallucination rate (88.0%) and tool-call correctness (88.3%) with reasoning enabled, outperforming Gemini‑3.1‑Pro and Claude‑4.6 on both measures. It also led on non-hallucination rate with reasoning off. Retell says new customers who choose OpenAI models continue using them. Customers also have access to OpenAI Realtime models through the Realtime API and OpenAI text-to-speech as a voice engine. Retell makes new OpenAI models available the day they launch and reruns its benchmark as models improve. Resolving more than 80% of customer requests with agents Contact center teams use Retell to define their agents’ business context, goals, and rules. Developers can further configure workflows and integrations. During a call, Retell’s harness runs the audio models, tracks the conversation, and manages when the agent speaks, listens, or responds to interruptions. OpenAI models, including GPT‑6.1 Sol, GPT‑6 Astra, and GPT‑5.5, use the customer’s instructions and conversation context to generate responses and request tool calls. Retell executes those calls through integrations with the customer’s systems and can write outcomes back to a customer record. When human judgment is needed, the voice agent briefs the representative with context, answers their follow-up questions, and confirms they are ready before connecting the customer. After a call, Retell uses GPT‑5.6 Terra to analyze the transcript and extract outcomes and other data. With this approach, Retell reports customer resolution rates above 80%. At Pine Park Health, Retell agents handle appointment reminders and proactive scheduling and record the outcomes in patients’ medical charts. The team recovered medical-assistant time equivalent to 2.1 full-time roles and redirected it to patient care. Its scheduling agent achieved a 55.7% booking rate when it reached a patient. To help keep calls responsive even at peak demand, Retell uses dedicated servers with OpenAI, shared across its customers. In Retell’s October 13, 2026 benchmark, GPT‑6 Astra and GPT‑6 Sol each recorded median first-sentence latency of 883 milliseconds, with GPT‑5.6 Luna ranking highest at 497 milliseconds. OpenAI’s regional API endpoints allow developers to route traffic to specific geographic regions, which helps Retell reduce network travel time and meet customers’ data residency requirements in industries such as healthcare and financial services. Retell also builds redundancy into each layer of the service. It runs its own telephony stack with a second stack ready to take over, along with backups for voice and model services, to help keep calls running when a service fails. Retell says earlier models could not perform this work reliably. Retell continues to hire, but its engineering team hasn’t needed to grow at the same pace as the business. Retell reports $1.6 million in annual revenue run rate per employee. “By using Codex, the engineering team has a virtual teammate that can pair with them on any kind of difficult task.” —Bing Wu, Co-founder and CEO, Retell AI Putting customer expertise to work Every business knows how it wants to serve its customers. Next, Retell plans to expand quality assurance and simulation, support more languages, and develop knowledge base auto-learning. As OpenAI models continue to advance, Retell expects its customers to deliver world-class service to millions of callers, on their own terms. “No one knows a company’s customers better than the company itself.
